# Briefs And Collaborative Editing

## Choose The Reader's Structure

For substantial research, deliver a brief document linked to a separate evidence
companion document, with a return link from the companion. Use the requested
platform and existing artifact skills. If no destination is established, resolve
that choice while continuing independent research. Do not create unsolicited
duplicates or publish to a substitute service when a destination is unavailable.

Default brief structures vary with purpose. For example:

| Purpose | Lead with | Add only as useful |
|---|---|---|
| Revisit a prior decision | Present conclusion, rationale, and what changed | Options, adoption conditions, decision timeline |
| Assess project outcomes | Results against intended outcomes and limits | Contributing factors, lessons, unresolved measurement |
| Reconstruct contributions | Work performed and supported attribution | Collaboration, impact, uncertainty, chronology |
| Recover prior work | What exists, relevance, and reuse constraints | Maturity, ownership, gaps, next investigation |
| Establish a historical account | What happened and how confidently it is known | Competing accounts, dated milestones, open questions |

Avoid separate summary and conclusion sections that repeat each other. Give a
reader who stops early the answer, why it matters, and the strongest reasons.
Use short paragraphs, selective bullets, meaningful bolding, and sparing callouts.
Increase specificity and density deeper in the document. Detailed tables,
chronology, search methods, and generation notes belong later or in the companion.
Use diagrams only when they clarify the evidence or comparison.
Check the agreed word budget before publication and the rendered page count when
specified. Count citations and appendices consistently with the task contract;
word count alone cannot establish a one-page layout.

Keep material uncertainty close to the claim it qualifies. A methodology appendix
is not a place to hide a limitation that could change the reader's decision.
Place citations beside the supported assertions, with locators when available.

The companion should contain the scope and coverage summary, search log, complete
candidate inventory with read coverage and one-line findings, fuller evidence and
analysis, competing explanations, and remaining gaps. Include a timeline or
options analysis when it answers the research question. Explain source access and
generation details there without making them the brief's opening story.
Include the final readable research plan, worklist states, and surrounding-record
follow-ups. Show which searches ran and which sources were inspected and analyzed;
distinguish a completed capped query from established coverage of a source corpus.

## Collaborate Without Losing Evidence

Before editing a shared document, read its current contents and compare against
the last verified version when available. Identify user changes and preserve their
intent and voice. If no earlier version exists, do not invent an edit history.
Keep revisions inside the user's authorized scope and requested review cadence.

Separate factual corrections, interpretive changes, and editorial preferences.
For a substantive change, ask whether it changes the comparison, time period,
population, certainty, attribution, or recommendation. Revisit only the evidence
needed to support that change, including expert context supplied by the user.
Offer a focused clarification when consequential ambiguity remains.

When a claim changes, check that its citations support the new meaning. A working
link or nearby related source is insufficient. Update the evidence companion's
claim mapping and research scope when additional investigation occurs. Do not
silently restore a caveat the user intentionally removed; explain any remaining
material evidentiary concern and resolve it within the current editing authority.

Batch coherent content revisions, then perform the editorial and presentation
passes below. Avoid repeated layout work on prose whose meaning is still changing.
Recheck after material edits or observed failures rather than rerunning unchanged
checks indefinitely.
Compare expected content with fresh exports, including every required candidate
row, cell and link occurrence. A tool's failed round-trip flag needs diagnosis;
an independent content comparison can resolve harmless serialization differences
but cannot establish visual fidelity. Do not silently discard the failed flag.
The [bundle helper](bundle-procedure.md) checks HTML exports; other formats need
equivalent artifact checks with explicit failure status. Semantic citation fit
and layout remain separate reviews.

## Read The Finished Brief As A Reader

After the last substantive change, read the complete brief in order, including
its title, opening, bullets, callouts, captions, and conclusion if present.

- Make the recommendation or finding immediately understandable. Use familiar
  verbs and name the proposed action; replace awkward phrasing and process jargon.
- Keep terminology consistent and sentences short enough to follow. Expand an
  unfamiliar acronym when it matters to the intended reader.
- Remove repeated conclusions, excessive qualification, and headings that promise
  a different point from the content beneath them. Keep necessary uncertainty.
- Check that bullets are parallel, emphasis identifies the strongest evidence,
  and readers can understand the opening without studying dense tables.

Do not improve fluency by strengthening an uncertain finding. After a wording
change, recheck the comparison, scope, attribution, qualifier, and citation fit.
Record this editorial review separately from the semantic evidence review.

Apply the same prose pass to the companion's narrative and authored summaries,
including dates, quantities, units, and sample descriptions. Dense evidence should
still use readable sentences and spacing. Preserve exact queries, identifiers,
source titles, and quotations; distinguish those records from prose you authored.

## Review The Presentation

Inspect the actual delivered rendering through the artifact workflow. Check
technical correctness and whether the whole document is comfortable to read:
coherent type and color, clear heading hierarchy, restrained emphasis, page density,
section and list breaks, and tables wide enough for their content. A document can
have no clipping and still be difficult to scan or visually inconsistent.
Inspect an overview and representative detail, including dense inventory pages
and boundaries between sections. Use extra checks where a defect appears.
Keep this review separate from content round-trip and citation checks; record the
rendered version inspected and any remaining presentation limitations.

## Finalization

Treat **review only**, **propose edits**, and **apply edits** as distinct scopes.
User finalization freezes further edits until reopened. A review-only request
permits reading and reporting findings, not repairing citations or formatting.

Before reporting an edited artifact complete, verify that the brief answers its
intended question, its strongest claims have appropriate evidence, unresolved
issues are visible, the companion reflects actual research, and the two documents
link correctly. Respect the user's final version and report any remaining concern
without assuming that finalization authorizes another revision.
