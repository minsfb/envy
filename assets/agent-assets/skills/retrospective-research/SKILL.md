# Research Past Work

Establish what the reader needs from the past, then investigate and explain it.
Default to **two linked documents: a concise brief and an evidence companion**.
Adapt the brief's structure to its purpose; a decision timeline is optional.
Respect a requested lighter response, different format, or review-only scope.

## Establish The Question

Reuse the conversation, known sources, and prior research before asking questions.
Clarify consequential gaps in reader, purpose, scope, and output. Ask a small
useful set at a time; no questionnaire quota or mandatory approval checkpoint.
Continue work that does not depend on an unanswered question.

Use [intake and research assignments](references/intake-and-assignments.md) when
scoping an investigation or preparing parallel work. Record the current reader
question, period and subject boundaries, people and aliases, source coverage,
known hypotheses, and requested artifact destination. Treat recollections as
leads and participant evidence, not conclusions the research must confirm.
Preserve explicit output requirements verbatim in a shared task contract,
including tables, length, editing scope, and protected artifacts. If brevity is
unspecified, choose a working word budget suited to the reader; a one-page request
also needs a rendered page check. Pass the contract itself through delegation.
Record whether earlier investigations may be reused. Prior evidence companions
and artifact graphs are useful Trunk sources for overlapping work; their copied
findings are historical assessments, not fresh inspections or independent proof.
An independent-discovery test explicitly excludes earlier outputs and their
lineage; ordinary research should not inherit that test exclusion.

Use available source and artifact skills for platform-specific operations.
This workflow supplies no provider, model, or access configuration. Research
permission is not permission to contact participants or change sharing settings.
Reuse existing authority for requested artifacts and revisions; do not repeatedly
ask for permission to perform work already authorized.

## Discover And Read

For new delegated or multi-wave investigations, use the shared SQLite graph in
the [graph workflow](references/graph-workflow.md). It records source identities,
captured evidence, actual inspections, scoped assessments, references, leads and
work ownership. Original operations and the budget recorder retain authority.
Workers use the helper; they do not edit SQLite or maintain competing inventories.
Use its preflight checks and [append-only repair procedure](references/graph-workflow.md#recover-originals-without-repeating-research)
when a saved index or return cannot normalize; preserve the original evidence and frontier.
Generated inventories and the evidence companion must describe one recorded
graph sequence. The graph makes omissions visible; it does not prove source
meaning, extraction completeness or search recall.

Before the first search, write a living [research plan](references/research-plan.md)
covering questions, terms and aliases, people, periods, source surfaces, and work
types. Turn it into a bounded worklist of concrete searches, with owners and
priorities. Include prototypes, reviews, feedback, measurements, and adjacent
records where relevant; topical titles alone may miss the decisive evidence.
Include organizing records such as project indexes, trackers, review schedules
and team landing pages when they can reveal the source network.
Interpret **Content**, **Critical/Review**, and **Trunk** as overlapping roles:
what was built or proposed; how it was tested, challenged or gated; and where
related work can be found. Default to reading plausible in-scope records in all
three roles. An organizing record need not state the eventual answer to matter.
Choose useful combinations instead of multiplying every term by every surface.
Pair precise searches with broad queries and competing explanations. Initial
reconnaissance is planned work too; use its discoveries to extend the plan.
Give important topic/work-type combinations concrete search items or an explicit
scope omission. Listing “reviews” in coverage is not a review search. Include a
broader route without person or current-project restrictions when a consequential
answer may be hidden under generic titles.

Use [search coverage and routes](references/search-routes.md) to map work types
to plausible artifact forms and locations, then to verified retrieval routes and
suitable query modes. Select complementary routes before results arrive; a
plausible result set can still omit pivotal sources. Use an available environment
source catalog for provider details, verifying its current capabilities and access.
Keep the selected branches in the living plan and generate concrete searches from
them; unsupported or omitted branches need explicit reasons. Expand by usefulness,
not every possible combination or a fixed repetition count.

Use [read-driven discovery waves](references/discovery-waves.md) to alternate
initial discovery, diverse seed reads, extraction and follow-up. Reserve effort
for reading and expansion before synthesis. Inspect actual query constraints:
an activity label in the plan does not make a restrictive title predicate cover
that activity. Where supported, include a useful topical body search without
requiring particular topic or activity words in the title.

Apply [research budgets and balanced expansion](references/research-budgets.md)
before dispatching work. Reserve capacity for at least two complete follow-up
cycles, and start them while baseline seed coverage continues. Check the graph
for unassigned terms and uninspected reference surfaces before deeper work. Use shared admission counters, distinct assessment identities,
research time accounting and ancestry across both links and new-term searches.
These limits constrain discovery, not the coordinator's synthesis, revision,
verification or delivery. Use the user's explicit effort constraints when given;
otherwise allow sustained work and report progress rather than rushing to finish.

Establish the actual capabilities of each source: search scope, pagination,
metadata, body, comments, revisions, slides, or visual frames/nodes. Report what
can be read and what remains inaccessible. A preview or extracted text is not
proof of full visual coverage. Retry an affected route when access changes;
reassess coverage and research needs without repeating unrelated searches.

Maintain [search, source, and evidence records](references/evidence-records.md)
from the first search. Include promising unread sources and seen-but-excluded
material. Ownership alone does not prove authorship or contribution.
For delegated or multi-wave work, use the
[evidence bundle procedure](references/bundle-procedure.md) to capture operations
as they happen and validate returns before synthesis. An existing recorder is
suitable if it preserves the same receipts and enforces the same acceptance
checks. Do not reconstruct missing retrieval history from memory.
Enable the helper's `research_plan`, `search_routes`, `discovery_followups` and
`research_budget` and `protected_followups`
requirements for new delegated or multi-wave investigations. Save the plan before execution and bind each operation to its
assigned item and graph assignment. Generate its readable working document from the same records;
do not maintain a second checklist that can drift from actual searches.

For broad investigations, delegation means actually launching subagents or
additional child agent processes with bounded assignments and collecting their
returns. Naming roles or doing their work sequentially in one agent is not fan-out.
Use delegated search and reading waves when the expected or observed workload
includes substantial search branches, large result sets or multiple substantial
document batches, and delegation is available and permitted. A single research
question can warrant several readers. Reassess after initial results; record a
concrete reason for a sequential fallback. Match concurrency to scope, available
capacity and user choices; do not require a fixed team or model.
Use one coordinating owner for shared decisions, the plan and document edits.
Register each real child actor and its parent assignment in the shared graph.
Workers can retain observations and propose follow-ups within their scope;
acceptance, global bounds and cross-branch reconciliation stay coordinated.
Researchers return evidence and limits, not just polished summaries. Reconcile
overlapping discoveries by stable source identity; agreement between agents is
not independent corroboration when they use the same underlying source.
Pass verified provider read methods and their limits in each assignment. A
convenient export does not override a provider's content-fidelity requirements.

Read decisive sources directly before relying on them in the brief. Reconcile
contradictions and investigate consequential gaps. User corrections and newly
available evidence can warrant a focused follow-up wave. Preserve useful findings
that fall outside the initial extraction categories instead of discarding them.
Record every discovered asset in the common inventory and distinguish capture,
actual reading or visual inspection, analysis, and use or exclusion. Use
[reading and reference expansion](references/reading-and-expansion.md) for reading
assignments: enumerate outgoing artifact references from accessible content before
selecting follow-up reads, retain their source locations and assess each candidate.
Also extract unlinked names, acronyms and surrounding-record leads. Queue useful
proposals, prototypes, reviews, feedback, decisions, implementation and measurements.
Between waves, reconcile receipts, inventory states, and these follow-ups. Resolve
high-priority pending work or explicitly bound it with reasons before synthesis.
For each analyzed source, record whether its vocabulary, links or context warrant
expansion. Turn consequential new terms and neighboring-record leads into assigned
work or explicit bounds. Retain each reader's original returned lead list and
reconcile it against graph obligations and every affected question; validating
only a selected summary cannot detect a dropped return. At each coverage checkpoint, use actual inspections to
choose the next work; a large candidate inventory is not evidence of saturation.
Schedule follow-ups in breadth-first waves across branches and questions. For a
relevant source, default to reading and assessing each exposed research-artifact
child that lacks sufficient current-scope coverage. Pursue useful unlinked terms
in the same waves. Irrelevant leaves can stop that subtree; unread, inaccessible
or cap-deferred records cannot. Depth, count and time bounds retain an explicit
frontier after protected baseline and priority work.

## Interpret In Context

Weight evidence by the question and time being investigated. Contemporaneous
records can establish historical intent; current implementation establishes
current behavior. A later retrospective may add perspective but cannot silently
replace the earlier record. Distinguish event date, document date, and revision.

Separate documented facts, participant accounts, analytical inferences, and
recommendations. Distinguish an idea, proposal, decision, deferral, implementation,
and measured outcome. Missing records do not prove that a discussion or decision
never happened. Conflicts may remain unresolved; explain their effect on the
conclusion instead of deciding by source count or author seniority.

Before comparing alternatives, identify the baseline, what changes, what stays
constant, and the outcome each metric measures. Check population, denominator,
task, period, and whether figures are targets or observations. A benefit relative
to one alternative may not explain a decision against another. General evidence
can support a qualified expectation without proving an untested comparison.

Use the user's expertise to improve the analysis while retaining provenance and
checking materially changed claims. Credit contributors and establish decision
authority where relevant; do not infer motives or approval from mere attendance,
document access, or an ambiguous acknowledgment.

## Synthesize And Refine

Read [briefs and collaborative editing](references/brief-and-editing.md) when
preparing or revising reader-facing work. Stabilize the evidence supporting a
claim before polishing it. New evidence can reopen the affected conclusions;
an earlier draft is not a fixed research outline or an independent source.

Lead the brief with the answer and why it matters to its readers. Bring forward
the strongest reasons and practical implications. Increase detail deeper in the
document, with detailed chronology and methodology in the companion or appendix.
Keep the companion aligned with the final claims and actual research coverage.
After the last substantive revision, read the complete brief for natural prose,
clear actions, consistent terminology, and unnecessary repetition. Apply the same
prose pass to the companion's narrative and authored summaries. Recheck any
changed claim's qualifiers and citations. Then review the rendered presentation
for hierarchy, emphasis, density, and readable tables as well as technical defects.

Read the current document before revising it. Preserve the user's changes and
voice; distinguish review, proposed edits, and authorized edits. Check citation
support after semantic changes, not merely that links exist. When the user
finalizes the document or requests review only, report observations without edits.

## Complete And Report

Judge coverage against the research question and domain, not against whether
enough sources support a draft conclusion. Review the source network and rejected
leaves independently of claim validation; organizing records can be relevant
because they locate other work. Derive and revise conclusions from that evidence.

Stop discovery when the agreed questions are answered or clearly bounded by remaining gaps,
key claims and counterevidence have been checked, and further searches within
the agreed scope are no longer materially changing the findings. A budget or
access limit can end a wave without establishing completeness. Report that limit
and the consequential remaining leads; do not promise unrestricted exhaustiveness.
An unanswered central question needs a coverage checkpoint: try relevant older
names, adjacent decision/review records, and linked artifacts; ask for known
source leads when useful. Distinguish failure to find the decision from evidence
that it was never made. Label a lead supplied later as assisted discovery.
Reconcile the final plan against executed searches, inspected and analyzed sources,
and unresolved record chains. Review unanswered questions, competing accounts,
unread consequential links and capped searches before recording the final coverage
checkpoint. Include the readable plan and coverage account in
the evidence companion. A completed query is not proof that a capped search
covered the corpus, and a completed checklist is not proof of complete discovery.
When a research cap is reached, stop admitting further discovery, retain the
frontier by priority and generation, and complete the deliverables from inspected
evidence. Describe incomplete baseline coverage as a budget or scheduling failure,
not a successful deeper investigation. Synthesis and the main author's rewriting
retain their own time; a discovery stop is never a reason to omit them.

Verify the delivered documents, their mutual links, citations, and coverage
records using the appropriate artifact tools. Consume validation results: failed
receipts or missing required artifact content keep that work incomplete. Export
graph views from one sequence and retain explicit semantic review against that
view; a valid export does not establish passage support or adequate discovery.
Export and compare actual content after publication; a successful write or generic
URL-access check alone is insufficient. Keep required evidence at an
authorized durable location; temporary extraction files must not be its only
record. Report the brief and companion links, material uncertainties, and any
requested work that remains unfinished. A handoff is separate and only needed
when continuation is requested.
